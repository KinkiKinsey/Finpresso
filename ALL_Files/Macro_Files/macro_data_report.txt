### **Current U.S. Economic Situation (June 2024 – May 2025)**  

The U.S. economy is showing signs of **strong but slowing growth**, with **persistent inflationary pressures** and **tight labor market conditions**. Here’s a breakdown of key trends and concerns:  

#### **Growth & Inflation**  
- **Real GDP growth** has moderated from **5.6% (Q2 2024) to 3.5% (Q1 2025)**, indicating a cooling but still robust expansion.  
- **PCE inflation (quarterly)** rose from **2.8% to 4.0% (Q3-Q4 2024)** before dropping sharply to **1.8% (Q1 2025)**, suggesting volatile price pressures.  
- **CPI (monthly)** continues to climb, reaching **320.8 in April 2025**, reflecting sticky core inflation despite Fed tightening.  
- **PPI (monthly)** has risen steadily, peaking at **148.4 in March 2025** before a slight dip, signaling lingering cost pressures in production.  

#### **Labor Market & Wages**  
- The **unemployment rate remains low (4.0%-4.2%)**, but has edged up slightly, hinting at softening demand.  
- **Average hourly earnings** keep rising (from **$35.0 to $36.06**), sustaining consumer spending but adding to wage-driven inflation risks.  
- The **personal savings rate has declined (4.8% to ~3.9%)**, suggesting households are dipping into savings amid high costs.  

#### **Monetary Policy & Financial Markets**  
- The **Fed Funds Rate has fallen from 5.33% (June 2024) to 4.33% (March 2025)**, indicating rate cuts to prevent a hard landing.  
- **Treasury yields (10Y-2Y spread)** turned positive in late 2024, reversing inversion, which may signal easing recession fears.  
- **Gold prices surged (~$2,327 to ~$3,305)**, reflecting investor hedging against inflation and economic uncertainty.  

#### **Housing & Trade**  
- **Home prices remain elevated (median ~$416,900 in Q1 2025)**, but **new home sales dipped (684K vs. 708K in Q3 2024)**, suggesting affordability strains.  
- **Import prices (152.8 in April 2025) outpace export prices (141.8)**, worsening trade balance pressures.  

### **Key Concerns**  
1. **Inflation Stickiness**: Despite PCE easing, CPI and PPI trends suggest underlying price pressures persist.  
2. **Slowing Growth Momentum**: GDP deceleration raises risks of a sharper downturn if Fed policy lags.  
3. **Consumer Strain**: Falling savings and rising wages may not fully offset high living costs.  
4. **Trade & Manufacturing Pressures**: Rising import costs could squeeze margins and fuel imported inflation.  

The Fed faces a **balancing act**—supporting growth while ensuring inflation doesn’t reignite. **Market volatility and housing affordability remain critical pressure points** in the near term.
