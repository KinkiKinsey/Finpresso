### **Current U.S. Economic Situation (May 2024 – April 2025)**  

The U.S. economy remains robust but faces mounting inflationary pressures and tightening financial conditions. **Real GDP growth** has slowed from 5.6% (early 2024) to 4.8% (early 2025), yet remains strong, indicating sustained economic expansion. However, **Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) inflation** has risen from 2.8% to 4.0%, while the **CPI** climbed from 314.069 in May 2024 to 319.799 by March 2025, signaling persistent inflation.  

The **labor market** remains tight, with the **unemployment rate fluctuating between 4.0% and 4.2%**, reflecting steady hiring but limited slack. **Average hourly earnings** rose from $34.89 to $36.00, fueling wage-driven inflation risks. Meanwhile, the **personal savings rate** dipped to 3.3% before recovering slightly, suggesting consumers are depleting buffers.  

The **Federal Reserve** has aggressively cut rates, with the **Fed Funds Rate dropping from 5.33% to 4.33%**, likely responding to slowing growth. However, long-term yields remain elevated—the **10Y Treasury yield peaked at 4.63%**—indicating lingering inflation expectations. The **10Y-2Y yield spread**, once deeply inverted (-43 bps), has turned positive (peaking at +36 bps), reducing recession fears but signaling higher borrowing costs.  

**Housing** shows mixed signals: **home prices (FHFA Index) rose steadily**, but **new home sales declined from 708K to 673K** before a slight rebound, reflecting affordability pressures. **Producer prices (PPI) and import costs surged**, with the **Import Price Index jumping from 148.8 to 152.4**, exacerbating cost-push inflation.  

**Key Concerns:**  
- **Sticky inflation** (PCE at 4.0%, CPI rising) may delay Fed easing.  
- **Slowing GDP growth** (down to 4.8%) suggests economic cooling.  
- **Persistent labor costs** (rising wages) could prolong inflation.  
- **Higher import prices** and **PPI increases** risk margin squeezes for businesses.  

The Fed faces a tough balancing act—supporting growth while preventing inflation from reigniting. If inflation does not ease further, financial conditions may tighten again, risking a sharper slowdown.
