### **Current U.S. Economic Situation (2024–2025) – Key Trends & Pressures**  

The U.S. economy remains robust but shows signs of cooling, with persistent inflationary pressures and tightening financial conditions.  

**Growth & Inflation:**  
- **Real GDP growth** slowed from **5.0% (early 2024) to 3.5% (mid-2025)**, indicating economic resilience but moderating expansion.  
- **PCE inflation** (Fed’s preferred gauge) rose from **3.7% to 4.0%** before sharply dropping to **1.8%**, suggesting volatile price trends.  
- **CPI inflation** continues climbing (from **314.54 to 320.795**), reflecting sticky core inflation despite Fed tightening.  
- **PPI** (producer costs) rose steadily (**144.869 to 148.374**) before a slight dip, signaling lingering supply-side pressures.  

**Labor Market & Wages:**  
- **Unemployment** remains low (**4.0%–4.2%**), but recent upticks hint at softening demand.  
- **Hourly earnings grew steadily (35.07 to 36.06)**, sustaining consumer spending but adding to cost pressures.  
- **Personal savings rate dropped (4.3% to 3.3%)**, suggesting households are depleting buffers amid high living costs.  

**Housing & Trade:**  
- **Home prices** peaked (**$419,300**) before easing slightly, while **new home sales declined (707K to 662K)**, reflecting mortgage rate impacts.  
- **Export/import price growth** stayed volatile, with imports (**149.2 to 152.8**) outpacing exports, worsening trade balances.  

**Financial Markets & Fed Policy:**  
- The **Fed cut rates aggressively (5.33% to 4.33%)**, yet long-term yields (**10Y at 4.28%**) remain elevated, tightening credit.  
- The **10Y-2Y yield spread inverted (-25bps) but turned positive (50bps)**, reducing recession fears but signaling uncertainty.  
- **Gold prices surged ($2,426 to $3,357)**, reflecting risk aversion and inflation hedging.  

**Key Concerns:**  
1. **Inflation Stickiness:** CPI and import prices remain elevated, complicating Fed policy.  
2. **Consumer Strain:** Falling savings and high debt servicing could weaken spending.  
3. **Housing Slowdown:** Higher rates are cooling sales, risking broader economic spillovers.  
4. **Global Pressures:** Rising import costs and volatile trade dynamics may squeeze margins.  

The Fed faces a delicate balance—easing policy to avoid a hard landing while preventing inflation resurgence.
