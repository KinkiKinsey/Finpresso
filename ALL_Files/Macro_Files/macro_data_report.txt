### Current U.S. Economic Situation (June 2024 – May 2025)  

The U.S. economy shows **strong but slowing GDP growth**, with Q2 2024 at **5.6%**, moderating to **3.5% by Q1 2025**. However, **declining PCE inflation (from 3.7% to 1.8%)** suggests weakening demand-side pressures, while **CPI remains elevated (319.8 in April 2025)**, indicating lingering cost pressures. The **labor market is tight (unemployment: 4.0–4.2%)**, but **slowing wage growth (avg. hourly earnings up only 3.0% YoY)** and a **falling personal savings rate (3.3–4.8%)** signal consumer strain.  

**Key Concerns:**  
1. **Inflation Stickiness vs. Demand Slowdown:** CPI remains high despite PCE cooling, complicating Fed policy.  
2. **Yield Curve Normalization:** The **10Y-2Y spread turned positive (50bps in May 2025)**, ending inversion but reflecting uncertainty.  
3. **Housing Market Stagnation:** Median home prices **peaked at $419.3K in Q3 2024**, then dipped slightly, while new home sales fluctuate weakly.  
4. **Trade Pressures:** Rising **import prices (152.4 vs. 148.4 in June 2024)** outpace **export growth (141.8 index)**, squeezing margins.  
5. **Fed Policy Lag Effect:** Fed funds rate cuts (**5.33% → 4.33%**) haven’t yet spurred GDP acceleration, risking **"soft landing" failure**.  

**Immediate Pressures:**  
- **Gold prices surged to $3,305 (May 2025)**, signaling investor hedging against stagflation risks.  
- **PPI uptrend (147.464 → 148.045)** suggests lingering producer-side inflation.  
- **Treasury yield volatility (10Y: 4.28%)** reflects mixed growth/inflation expectations.  

**Bottom Line:** The economy is **cooling unevenly**—strong GDP but weakening consumption, sticky inflation, and financial market skepticism. The Fed must balance **growth support** against **inflation persistence**, with risks tilted toward **mild stagflation** if demand falters further.
